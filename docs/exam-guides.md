# Exam guides

For each exam: books in order, how to study, how to practise, and where to find real past papers. Same content as the Exam guides tab in Study Desk.

## SSC CGL

**Books, in order**

1. NCERT Maths Class 6 to 10 (free)
2. Rakesh Yadav, Class Notes: Arithmetic, then Advance Maths
3. R.S. Aggarwal, Verbal & Non-Verbal Reasoning
4. Neetu Singh, Plinth to Paramount
5. Lucent’s General Knowledge + Lucent’s Computer
6. Kiran, SSC CGL previous papers

**How to study**

1. Months 1 to 2: NCERT maths, then Arithmetic chapters one by one
2. Months 3 to 4: Advance Maths (geometry, trigonometry, algebra)
3. Daily: 1 reasoning chapter, 1 English grammar rule, 1 Lucent chapter on the bus
4. Make a formula note for every maths chapter in Notes

**How to practise**

1. After each chapter: solve all its questions, then 20 past-paper questions on it
2. Speed drills every day: squares, cubes, fraction percentages
3. From month 5: one sectional test a day, one full mock every Sunday
4. Analyse each mock: every wrong answer becomes a note or flashcard

**Remember:** Tier 1 has 0.50 negative marking, so attempt only what you can solve. GA is the fastest section, finish it first.

**Real past papers**

- [SSC answer keys with question papers](https://ssc.gov.in) — SSC posts each exam’s questions with the answer key after the exam. Open Answer Keys on the site.

## IBPS PO

**Books, in order**

1. Rakesh Yadav Arithmetic (shared with SSC)
2. Arun Sharma, Quantitative Aptitude (DI chapters)
3. M.K. Pandey, Analytical Reasoning
4. S.P. Bakshi, Objective General English
5. RBI and PIB monthly for banking awareness (free)

**How to study**

1. Arithmetic first, then DI, because most DI is arithmetic in tables and charts
2. Reasoning: puzzles and seating every single day, they are half the section
3. English: read one editorial a day, note 5 words into Flashcards
4. Banking awareness: 20 minutes daily from RBI and PIB updates

**How to practise**

1. Prelims is 20 min per section: practise with the timer at exactly 20 min
2. Solve 3 puzzles and 2 DI sets daily
3. Two prelims mocks a week in the last 3 months
4. For Mains descriptive: write one essay a week and check it on Write & Improve

**Remember:** Sectional time limits mean you must clear every section. Never leave a section to the end.

**Real past papers**

- [IBPS official site](https://www.ibps.in) — IBPS does not publish past papers. Free memory-based papers are on prep sites; use IndiaBIX for topic practice.
- [IndiaBIX topic practice](https://www.indiabix.com)

## AFCAT

**Books, in order**

1. Arihant Pathfinder AFCAT
2. NCERT Maths Class 6 to 10 (free)
3. Lucent’s General Knowledge (shared)
4. AFCAT past papers and the official mock on afcat.cdac.in
5. EKT: your GATE CS books (digital, networks, COA)
6. N.K. Natarajan, SSB Interview: The Complete Guide

**How to study**

1. Numerical ability = SSC arithmetic, so it is already covered
2. Military aptitude and spatial: practise the figure questions in Pathfinder daily
3. GK: defence news, awards, sports and current affairs every week
4. EKT: revise GATE digital logic, COA and networks

**How to practise**

1. Full 2-hour paper every weekend in exam conditions
2. Mark only sure answers: +3 right, minus 1 wrong
3. Start SSB prep the day you apply: OIR tests, PPDT stories, WAT words daily
4. Run and do push-ups 5 days a week for the SSB

**Remember:** Final-year students can apply. Two attempts a year, Feb and Aug.

**Real past papers**

- [AFCAT official site](https://afcat.cdac.in) — Has the official sample questions and the online mock test.

## CDS

**Books, in order**

1. Arihant Pathfinder CDS
2. NCERT Maths Class 6 to 10 (free)
3. Rakesh Yadav Advance Maths (shared with SSC)
4. Lucent’s GK + NCERT Class 6 to 10 science and social
5. UPSC CDS past papers (free on upsc.gov.in)
6. N.K. Natarajan, SSB Interview guide

**How to study**

1. Maths is the deciding paper. Cover NCERT 6 to 10 fully first
2. GK: history, polity and geography from NCERT, current affairs weekly
3. English: same as SSC, plus reading one long article a day

**How to practise**

1. Solve 10 years of CDS papers, one per weekend
2. Each paper is 2 h with minus 1/3: practise skipping hard questions
3. SSB prep in parallel, same as AFCAT

**Remember:** Final-year students can apply for IMA, INA and AFA. OTA needs only English and GK.

**Real past papers**

- [UPSC previous question papers](https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers) — Pick Combined Defence Services, then the year.

## UPSC CSE Prelims

**Books, in order**

1. NCERT Class 6 to 12: History, Geography, Polity, Economics, Science (free)
2. M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity
3. Spectrum, A Brief History of Modern India
4. G.C. Leong, Physical and Human Geography
5. Ramesh Singh, Indian Economy + Economic Survey (free)
6. Shankar IAS Environment; Nitin Singhania Art & Culture
7. UPSC past papers (free)

**How to study**

1. Read all NCERTs once before any reference book
2. Then one reference book per subject, read twice
3. Current affairs daily from PIB and one newspaper, make monthly notes
4. Keep notes short. Revise them every Sunday

**How to practise**

1. After each subject: solve UPSC past questions of that subject
2. Weekly CSAT practice. It needs 33% and many fail it
3. Full prelims mocks from 4 months before the exam
4. Mains needs answer writing: start only after prelims basics are solid

**Remember:** Choose UPSC only as a full-time 1 to 2 year plan. Decide after your final year.

**Real past papers**

- [UPSC previous question papers](https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers) — Pick Civil Services (Preliminary), then the year.

## GATE CS

**Books, in order**

1. Algorithms: CLRS
2. OS: OSTEP (free) or Galvin
3. DBMS: Korth
4. Networks: Kurose & Ross
5. TOC: Peter Linz
6. Compilers: Aho & Ullman
7. COA: Hamacher; Digital: Morris Mano
8. Discrete maths: Kenneth Rosen
9. NPTEL lectures + GATE Overflow (free)

**How to study**

1. Order: Discrete maths and DSA, then OS, DBMS, CN, then COA, Digital, TOC, Compilers
2. One subject at a time: NPTEL lecture, then the book chapter, then notes
3. General Aptitude is 15 marks and shares prep with SSC, keep it light

**How to practise**

1. Finish each subject with its GATE Overflow past questions (10 years)
2. Keep a mistakes notebook per subject
3. Last 3 months: full timed papers twice a week
4. Practise NAT questions on the virtual calculator, they have no negative marks

**Remember:** Sit GATE 2027 in 3rd year for practice. The score lasts 3 years for PSUs and M.Tech.

**Real past papers**

- [GATE Overflow](https://gateoverflow.in) — Every GATE CS question since the 1990s, sorted by subject and topic, with solutions.
- [GATE 2027 official site](https://gate2027.iitm.ac.in) — Official past papers and the mock test interface.

## Placement (my plan)

**Books, in order**

1. Striver A2Z DSA sheet (free)
2. LeetCode Top Interview 150 (free)
3. OSTEP, Korth, Kurose & Ross (shared with GATE)
4. Cracking the Coding Interview
5. TryHackMe, PortSwigger Academy (free)
6. R.S. Aggarwal Quantitative Aptitude or IndiaBIX

**How to study**

1. One DSA topic a day in Block B, in Striver order
2. CS core alongside your semester courses
3. Cyber: one TryHackMe room or PortSwigger lab every weekend
4. Build 2 projects: e.g. a vulnerability scanner and a log analyser in Python

**How to practise**

1. 2 LeetCode problems a day, review patterns weekly
2. Aptitude test on IndiaBIX twice a week
3. Mock interviews with friends from 2 months before
4. Join CTFs on CTFtime with your college team

**Remember:** Put your projects and CTF results on GitHub and your resume. They matter more than marks for cyber roles.

**Real past papers**

- [LeetCode](https://leetcode.com) — Search the topic name under Problems, filter by tag.
- [IndiaBIX](https://www.indiabix.com) — Placement aptitude questions by topic.
